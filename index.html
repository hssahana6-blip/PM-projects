Chapter 8 — How to Make a Habit Irresistible

In the 1940s, a Dutch scientist named Nikolaas Tinbergen performed a series of experiments that would forever change our understanding of what motivates behaviour.

Tinbergen—who would later receive the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine—was studying herring gulls. He noticed that when a mother gull returned to the nest, the baby gulls would peck at a red spot on her beak to encourage her to regurgitate food.

The more exaggerated the stimulus, the more irresistible it became. A wooden beak with three red dots triggered more pecking than a real beak with one. A giant head with an enormous red spot — something that had never existed in nature — triggered even more.

Scientists refer to these invented stimuli as supernormal stimuli. A supernormal stimulus is a heightened version of reality that elicits a stronger response than the original thing.

Humans are no different. Modern food companies, social media platforms, and entertainment services are all in the business of creating supernormal stimuli — products so engineered for pleasure that our ancient reward systems can barely resist them.

The more dopamine that is released by an activity, the stronger the craving to repeat it. This is why junk food is hard to stop eating. This is why social media pulls us back in. And this is why making habits attractive is one of the most important things you can do if you want to build new ones.
