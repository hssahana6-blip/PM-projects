## Problem

There is a problem in the industry today: the people most willing to pay for a reading app are the least likely to get value from the ones that exist.

In Rajan's own words:

> *"I'd pay for anything that actually makes me read more. I've just never found one that works past the first week."*

Rajan has paid for Audible, Kindle Unlimited, and Blinkist. He barely uses any of them. He does not have a content problem. He has a habit problem — and every product he's bought has sold him content access when what he needed was behaviour change.

He has downloaded five reading apps. All of them measured what he read. None of them changed how often he read. He got a week of enthusiasm and then a quiet return to the same pattern.

The deepest problem is not practical — it is identity. Rajan wants to be the kind of person who reads. His manager reads constantly. He believes it is part of why she is good at her job. A reading app that only gives him a shelf full of books he hasn't opened does not solve that problem. It deepens it.

**The purchase barrier:** Rajan's primary objection is not price. It is: *"another app I'll use for a week and forget about."* [ASSUMPTION] He will pay $8–12/month for something that works — but he will not pay for something he has already failed to use. The conversion window is 7–14 days. If Readio has not changed his behaviour by then, he will not upgrade.

---

## Solution

The ideal solution for Rajan is a product that demonstrates value before it asks for money — and measures value the way he actually experiences it.

He does not experience value as "access to 3 million books." He experiences it as: *I read yesterday. And the day before. And I finished something for the first time in months.*

Readio solves the purchase decision by:
- Delivering a tangible early win within the first session — something finished, something tracked, a streak started
- Measuring habit consistency so Rajan can see week-over-week progress in terms he understands
- Removing the Goodreads import barrier so switching costs nothing and costs him no history
- Pricing the habit layer, not the content — positioning itself as a behaviour-change tool, not a content subscription
- Providing a weekly Reading Receipt that Rajan can point to and say: *"I read 47 minutes last week. That's 4× more than the month before."*

The conversion from free to paid happens when Rajan believes the habit is forming — and Readio's job is to make that belief possible within 14 days.

## Product Description

Readio is a reading habit app that turns reading intentions into measurable behaviour. Unlike content-access apps, Readio focuses entirely on habit consistency — tracking sessions, celebrating progress, and delivering a weekly Reading Receipt that shows users how much their reading life has changed. *(44 words)*

---

## Problem-Oriented Capabilities

1. **Early win mechanics** — delivers a meaningful progress signal within the first 10 minutes of use, so Rajan's first session answers the question "will this actually work?" before he has to commit

2. **Habit measurement, not content measurement** — tracks reading consistency (sessions, streaks, weekly minutes) rather than books-per-year, so Rajan can see the behaviour change he's paying for

3. **Frictionless switching** — imports Goodreads reading history on day one, so there is no cost to trying Readio alongside or instead of any existing app

4. **The Reading Receipt** — produces a weekly summary of reading activity across all formats, giving Rajan a concrete, shareable record of his progress that justifies the subscription each week

5. **Proportional pricing** — priced as a behaviour-change tool (~$8–12/month), not as a content subscription, so the value proposition is "this changes how often you read" rather than "this gives you access to books you already have"
