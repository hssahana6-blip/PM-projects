## 1. Value Proposition

**Market opportunity (problem):**
26% of US adults — approximately 65 million people — read zero books in the prior year (Pew Research, 2023). The reading app market has grown to serve avid readers extremely well: Goodreads has 150 million registered users, StoryGraph reached 5 million in January 2026, and Readwise charges $120/year to knowledge workers who read to retain. None of these products addresses the person who wants to read more but cannot sustain the habit.

The problem is urgent (people feel it daily), pervasive (65M+ in the US alone, hundreds of millions globally), and willing-to-pay — the self-improvement app market proves this. Calm and Headspace each generate $150M+ in annual revenue solving a comparably invisible daily habit problem (mindfulness) at $70/year. Duolingo's 47% DAU/MAU ratio demonstrates that a habit-first, progress-visible product can retain at scale. [ASSUMPTION — direct willingness-to-pay for Readio unvalidated]

The three problems that clear the urgent + pervasive + willing-to-pay test (from Market Problems Table, Stage 01):
- "Days go by and I never open a book." (Priority: 25 — highest)
- "I give up mid-book — I don't feel like I'm making progress." (Priority: 20)
- "Genre filters don't match how I feel — I can't find what I want to read." (Priority: 20)

**Buyer persona:** Rajan, the Self-Improvement Seeker — pays for tools that make him better, has already spent money on Audible/Blinkist/Kindle Unlimited without getting the habit he wanted. Conversion window: 7–14 days. Price ceiling: ~$8–12/month. [ASSUMPTION]

**User persona:** Priya, the Lapsed Reader — used to read, stopped, carries guilt about it. Responds to non-judgmental progress signals. Will pay if the habit is visibly forming. Buyer and user are often the same person in this B2C product.

**Positioning statements (from Stage 06):**
- *For lapsed readers like Priya, Readio rebuilds the reading habit by making every session feel achievable and every week feel like progress.* (24 words)
- *For self-improvement seekers like Rajan, Readio turns the intention to read more into a measurable weekly habit — starting in the first session.* (25 words)

**Strategic alignment:**
Readio's strategy is to own the habit layer of the reading market — the segment no competitor has built for — and monetise it as a subscription behaviour-change tool. This is the same strategic position Duolingo holds in language learning and Headspace holds in mindfulness: not the content owner, but the habit engine. Long-term, Readio's data on reading behaviour (what people actually read, when, for how long, and what makes them come back) is a strategic asset no competitor currently holds for the lapsed-reader segment.

## 3. Value Capture

### Market sizing

**TAM (Total Addressable Market):**
Adults who read 0–3 books per year and express a desire to read more.
- US: ~65M adults read 0 books/year (Pew 2023); conservatively, ~30% desire to change = ~20M potential users in the US alone
- Global English-speaking markets (UK, AU, CA, IN): ~3× US = ~60M potential users
- **TAM: ~80M users globally** [ASSUMPTION — willingness-to-pay within this group unvalidated]

**SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):**
Adults with smartphones, $5–15/month discretionary app spend, and some prior reading intention.
- Estimate: 15% of TAM = ~12M users [ASSUMPTION]
- Benchmark: Headspace reached ~2M paid subscribers from a comparable TAM in mindfulness

**SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market — Year 3):**
- Conservative target: 0.5% of SAM = ~60,000 paid subscribers by end of Year 3
- Stretch target: 1.5% of SAM = ~180,000 paid subscribers by end of Year 3
[ASSUMPTION — no acquisition channel data yet]

## 4. Buy, Build or Partner

**Decision tree walkthrough:**

**Step 1 — Does the solution leverage Readio's distinctive competencies?**
Yes. All three distinctive competencies (non-reader onboarding, behaviour-change metrics, Reading Receipt) are core product experiences that must be built to be differentiated. They cannot be bought off the shelf because no product like Readio currently exists.
→ **Build.**

**Specific build vs. partner decisions within the product:**

| Component | Decision | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Core habit engine (streak, goals, receipt) | **Build** | Distinctive competency — must be proprietary |
| Mood-based discovery | **Build** | Core UX differentiator; StoryGraph shows it can be done with in-house curation + tagging |
| Book catalog / metadata | **Partner** | Integrate Open Library API or Google Books API — no need to build or maintain a catalog |
| Ebook reading experience | **Partner** | Deep-link to Kindle / Apple Books for actual reading; Readio tracks the session, not the content |
| Audiobook integration | **Partner (future)** | Audible or Libro.fm API — cross-format without owning audio infrastructure |
| Goodreads import | **Build** (thin integration) | Critical for switching; Goodreads has a public API; low build cost |
| Payments / subscriptions | **Partner** | RevenueCat or Stripe — industry standard; no need to build |
| Analytics / data pipeline | **Partner** | Amplitude or Mixpanel for product analytics; PostHog for free tier |

**Overall recommendation: Build the habit layer. Partner for content, catalog, and infrastructure.**

## 6. Recommendation

**GO WITH CONDITIONS.**

The opportunity is real: 65 million US adults read zero books per year, no reading app is built for them, and the self-improvement subscription market has demonstrated willingness to pay $70–120/year for habit-change tools. The competitive gap is defensible because habit mechanics are hard to retrofit into catalog-first products. The financial model reaches contribution-positive in Year 3 at conservative assumptions requiring just 15,000 paid subscribers — 0.02% of the addressable market.

**Conditions to proceed:**

1. **Validate willingness-to-pay before building.** Run a no-code landing page with a $8.99/month "join the waitlist" CTA. If 3%+ of visitors click through to pay intent, the model holds. Target: 500 landing page visitors, minimum 15 soft conversions.

2. **Achieve Day-7 retention ≥ 35% in closed beta before scaling.** Build the P0 feature set (mood onboarding, 10-minute goal, Reading Receipt, Goodreads import, progress bar) and release to 200 invited users. Do not begin paid acquisition until D7 retention is confirmed. This is the single most important leading indicator for LTV.

3. **Complete 10 primary market interviews before locking product spec.** The entire dossier rests on secondary research. Ten conversations with real lapsed readers will either confirm the assumptions (reducing investor risk) or reveal a more specific problem worth solving (improving the product). This is 2–3 weeks of work and the highest-ROI activity available before any code is written.

**If all three conditions are met:** proceed to Series A raise targeting $600K–$800K, allocate 60% to engineering build, 25% to growth, 15% to operations. Target contribution-positive by Month 30.

**If conditions are not met:** the assumptions are wrong and the model needs revision before investment. This is not a no-go — it is a signal to find the more specific problem the lapsed reader will actually pay to solve.
