## Distinctive Competencies

Attributes that are **valued AND materially different AND have at least an early proof signal:**

**1. The only reading app built for people who don't yet have a reading habit.**
No competitor onboards from zero. Every app — Goodreads, StoryGraph, Bookly — assumes the user already reads and needs tracking or discovery. Readio's entire product is built around the problem that comes before reading: becoming someone who reads. *Early proof: prototype validated; competitive gap confirmed across 6 major competitors.*

**2. Behaviour-change metrics over consumption metrics.**
Competitors measure what you read (books, pages, hours). Readio measures how consistently you read — streaks, momentum, session frequency. This shifts the product's promise from "access to content" to "change in your life." *Early proof: Duolingo's DAU/MAU ratio (~47%) vs. Goodreads' engagement patterns proves the model works at scale for habit formation. [ASSUMPTION — not yet proven for Readio specifically]*

**3. The Reading Receipt — a weekly moment that makes you feel like a reader.**
No competitor produces a warm, celebratory, non-judgmental weekly summary. The closest analogue is Spotify Wrapped — a moment users look forward to and share. For Readio, this runs weekly rather than annually, and is the primary retention mechanism. *Early proof: prototype feature designed and built; investor response positive. No user testing yet. ## What Readio must NOT try to compete on

- **Social network / community** — Goodreads' 150M users and Fable's book clubs are moats. Readio's social layer should be thin and opt-in, never performative.
- **Content catalog** — Amazon, Apple, Kobo own distribution. Readio partners with them; it does not compete.
- **Power-user depth** — Readwise owns the knowledge worker who reads to retain information. Readio serves the person who just wants to read more.
